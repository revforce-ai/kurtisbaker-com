## 1. How to read this document

Two proven methods are stacked on top of each other here:

- **Brunson's Offer Stack** answers *"Is this worth far more than the price?"*
  You list every deliverable, attach an honest value to each, sum to a large
  total, anchor against that total, then reveal a price that feels like a
  steal — wrapped in a guarantee and a reason to act now.
- **McCall Jones' REP** answers *"Do I believe it, and do I believe it for
  ME?"* Every element is pressure-tested through three lenses:
  - **R — Realization:** the belief shift / the "aha" the prospect must have
    before the offer even makes sense to them.
  - **E — Effort:** proof of the work, depth, and rigor behind it — *and* how
    much effort it removes from the prospect's plate.
  - **P — Proof:** the evidence that it works and that it works for people like
    them (credentials, process, results, third-party validation).

The order matters. **Realization opens the mind, Effort justifies the value,
Proof closes the gap.** Brunson tells you *what* to stack; REP tells you *how to
make each layer land.*

## 2. The audience & the core problem

The CWMI buyer is a successful owner of a small or middle-market business.
On paper they're winning. Underneath, three quiet fears run the show:

1. **"My net worth is trapped inside my business."** Most of their wealth is
   illiquid, undiversified, and tied to an asset they can't easily sell.
2. **"The business can't run — or sell — without me."** They own a high-paying
   job, not a transferable asset.
3. **"No one is looking at the whole board."** They have a CPA, maybe a broker,
   maybe an attorney — all in separate silos, none accountable for the outcome.

The transformation CWMI sells is not "returns." It is **freedom** — a life and
an estate that work *with* or *without* the business, on the owner's terms.
That word — *freedom* — is the throughline of Kurt's whole ecosystem (Freedom
Ready Business), and it anchors this offer.

## 8. Ethical urgency (the "act now")

No fake countdown timers — they'd erode the trust an RIA depends on. Use *true*
scarcity:

- **Capacity:** A fiduciary planning practice can only take on a limited number
  of new owner-families per quarter to maintain service quality. State the real
  number.
- **The cost of waiting is real and quantifiable:** Every year an exit is
  unplanned is a year of value left on the table and a tax bill left
  un-optimized. The market doesn't wait for the owner to be ready.
- **Time-in-market, not timing:** The earlier the plan starts, the more options
  stay open. Readiness is built over years, not weeks.

> "The best time to plan your exit was five years before you sell. The second
> best time is before you read the rest of this sentence."

## 9. Compliance guardrails (READ BEFORE PUBLISHING)

CWMI is a **state-registered RIA (New Jersey Bureau of Securities)** today. The
**SEC Marketing Rule does not technically govern a state adviser** — but NJ's
advertising rules (built on the NASAA model rule) and the Investment Advisers
Act **§206 anti-fraud provisions (which apply to *every* adviser, state or SEC)**
do. **Build to the stricter SEC Marketing Rule standard now anyway:** it keeps
this asset forward-compatible for the planned SEC transition (~12–24 months) so
nothing has to be torn down and rebuilt at the $100M threshold. Non-negotiables
before anything ships:

1. **No performance or return promises.** Nothing here may imply investment
   returns. Guarantees cover *process/experience* only (see §7). (Anti-fraud
   §206 — applies regardless of registration tier.)
2. **Substantiate every value figure** in §4–§6, or relabel them clearly as
   illustrative estimates of comparable standalone costs. Do not present a
   fictitious "discount."
3. **Testimonials/endorsements** (if added): disclose whether the person is a
   client, whether they were compensated, and any material conflict — *clearly
   and prominently*, not buried in a footnote.
4. **Required disclosures:** "Not investment, tax, or legal advice"; link to
   **Form ADV (Parts 2A/2B)** filed via NJ/IARD; fiduciary statement; firm
   **IAPD** link. (Form CRS/Part 3 is an SEC-registration requirement — add it
   at the SEC transition, not before, unless NJ requires it.)
5. **Credentials:** keep verification links live (cfp.net, EPI, Fi360) — they
   are the strongest *Proof* asset and an E-E-A-T signal.
6. **NJ-specific review:** confirm the asset against current NJ Bureau of
   Securities advertising/recordkeeping rules; retain copies of all advertising.
7. **Legal counsel review** of the final asset for the firm's jurisdiction
   before any public use.
